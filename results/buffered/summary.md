# Spatially separated small-data evaluation (results/buffered, `scripts/buffered_smalldata.py`)

Question: are the small-data advantages of the foundation models interpolation artefacts? Florida = committed table, ring negatives, `no_landcover` (49 factors); Minnesota = shared public-layer schema `sat_nolc` (34). Models: xgboost (hand-set), xgboost_tuned (nested spatial CV, inner groups = 25 km blocks of the training rows), random_forest, TabPFN-3.5, TabICLv2, Kumo-Tabular (fp32, full context). The pair of a positive = its nearest ring negative (`pairs_<region>.csv`).


## 1. Experiment A — county few-shot with a train–test buffer

Counties with ≥ 100 positives; training = k in-county positives + k in-county ring negatives; 3 seeds; the same fitted model is scored once and evaluated on the in-county rows farther than B km from every training row (B = 0 is the committed design) and on the `neighbours` test (other counties within 50 km of a training row). `paired` = the k positives' nearest ring negatives (random in-county fallback where the pair is outside the county or shared); `independent` = positives and negatives drawn independently. Cells with < 20 positives or negatives are skipped. Δ bands are descriptive: pairs share counties and seeds.
